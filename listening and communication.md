# Listening and Active Communication

## Q1. Steps for Active Listening

1. Give full attention. Put the phone away.
2. Don't interrupt.
3. Show you're listening with eye contact and nodding.
4. Say small things like "yes" or "okay".
5. Repeat in your own words what you heard.
6. Ask questions if something is not clear.
7. Don't judge. Keep an open mind.

## Q2. Fisher's model of Reflective Listening

- Listen more, talk less.
- Focus on the speaker's feelings, not general ideas.
- Repeat their message in your own words to check it.
- Don't argue or give advice.
- Notice their emotions and body language.
- Ask again if anything is unclear.

## Q3. Obstacles in my listening

- Phone and notifications.
- Thinking about my reply while the other person is talking.
- Getting impatient.
- Feeling tired or stressed.
- Mind wandering in long meetings.

## Q4. How I can improve my listening

- Keep my phone away during talks.
- Wait a moment before replying.
- Repeat back what I heard.
- Take notes in meetings.
- Be patient and ask questions.

## Q5. When I go Passive

When I want to avoid a fight, or when I'm not sure my opinion is right. I just say "okay, fine" and stay quiet.

## Q6. When I go Aggressive

When I'm stressed, under a deadline, or when someone keeps ignoring me. My tone gets sharp and I interrupt.

## Q7. When I go Passive Aggressive

When I'm upset but don't want to say it directly. I give short replies, stay silent, or make sarcastic comments.

## Q8. How to make my communication assertive

- Stop and think about what I really feel.
- Say it clearly and politely.
- Use "I" statements, like "I feel stressed when the plan changes".
- Keep my voice calm and make eye contact.
- Say "no" politely when needed.
- Speak up early, before I get angry.
- Listen to the other side too.
